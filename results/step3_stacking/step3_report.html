Branch B · Step 3 — Stacking & the frontier

Do the two levers compound — and where does the frontier reach?

The size × resolution grid ({giant, nested-giant} × {392, 448, 504}, 3 repeats, frozen keyframes) tests whether cutting both knobs at once still holds accuracy, and the Pareto scatter marks how far left of the nested-giant @504 default the frontier reaches.

The finding in one line

The levers do not compound on the indoor datasets — size dominates and the both-cut corner collapses — so the best config is just the nested-giant resolution knee. Only on UAS does a cheaper point (giant@448) actually beat the default.

The one place the levers compound favourably. giant@448 (5.8 %) Pareto-dominates the nested-giant @504 default (6.7 %): better accuracy at 35% less backbone compute and 2.3 GB less memory. giant is also flatter across resolution than nested (which spikes to 9.8 % at 448). Synthesis — Branch B closes

  • No general accuracy-neutral knee exists. Across Steps 1–3 and three datasets, neither resolution nor model size — alone or stacked — yields a config that is much cheaper at essentially the default ATE everywhere. The indoor frontier is dominated by the single nested-giant architecture; shrinking it collapses accuracy.
  • UAS is the lone exception, and a soft one. giant@448 beats the default there (−35% compute, better %-of-path ATE), but UAS is the bistable domain where held-out validation previously overturned a giant win — so it is a candidate operating point, not a safe default.
  • Decision. Keep nested-giant @504 as the baseline (or @448 on latency-critical indoor deployments). Static reduction is not the free win; the real speedup must come from temporal reuse (Branch C). Every later experiment is reported on top of this baseline.
  • Caveats. Single server GPU (RTX 3090-class), so latency/memory are server-only. Repeat band ~10⁻⁵–10⁻³ (bf16), below every gap. UAS ATE is % of trajectory length; campus_fog is a held-out platform.
