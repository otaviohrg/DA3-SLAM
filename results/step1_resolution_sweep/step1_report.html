Branch B · Step 1 — Resolution sweep

Does lowering input resolution buy speed without breaking the trajectory?

Fixed backbone nested-giant, resolutions {336, 392, 448, 504}, 3 repeats on frozen keyframes. Headline metric is the Sim3-aligned ATE (monocular). The answer depends entirely on the domain.

The finding in one line

The "free resolution knee" does not generalize. It exists on TUM, vanishes on Replica, and fragments into bistable failure on UAS — so static resolution reduction is a domain-specific lever, not a safe default cut.

ATE is shown as a percentage of trajectory length — the honest metric at km-scale, where a fixed metre error means very different things on a 1275 m tunnel versus a 670 m walk. Neither free nor monotonic: the aggregate zig-zags (9.1 → 7.9 → 9.8 → 6.7 %) because each sequence peaks at a different resolution and the tunnels flip between a ~6 % tracking mode and a ~16 % drift mode at adjacent resolutions. 504 is best overall, but 392 reaches 7.9 % at 43% less backbone compute. Map-detail was not collected — km-scale clouds are infeasible to dump and Chamfer at this RAM budget.

Synthesis & caveats

  • One knee does not fit three domains. TUM's tolerance for low resolution is a property of near-field textured desk scenes, not a general SLAM fact. Replica (clean geometry, wide depth range) and UAS (fisheye, km-scale, self-similar tunnels) both punish resolution cuts.
  • Trajectory–map dissociation is real where measured. On TUM and Replica the point cloud coarsens smoothly with lower resolution; on TUM the trajectory ignores it, on Replica it follows — the dissociation is dataset-dependent too.
  • Caveats. Single server GPU (RTX 3090-class) — no Jetson, so latency/memory are server-only. Repeat band is ~10⁻⁵ m (bf16), far below every reported gap. UAS map-detail omitted (km-scale clouds vs 30 GB RAM). UAS campus_fog is a held-out platform (UniPilot handheld).
